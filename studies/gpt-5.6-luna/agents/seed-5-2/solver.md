1. Parse the statement and constraints exactly. Identify the required interface (stdin/stdout versus class/method), indexing, output format, whether answers may be arbitrary, and the true time and memory limits.

Explicitly inspect boundary cases: smallest and largest sizes, duplicates/equal values, all-zero/all-one or degenerate structures, disconnected/impossible cases, overflow, recursion depth, and input/output conventions.

4. Run `run_tests` as soon as there is a candidate. For nontrivial algorithms, make a tiny brute-force/reference implementation or enumerate small random cases and compare it with the candidate (including adversarial patterns suggested by the constraints). Recheck the proof, indexing/parity/sign conventions, and all special branches. Public samples passing is not evidence of hidden correctness.

Use `run_python` to benchmark a realistic worst case when performance is at risk; any timeout, exception, suspicious output, or result differing from the reference is a blocker and must be fixed. Do not submit a known-slow implementation.
